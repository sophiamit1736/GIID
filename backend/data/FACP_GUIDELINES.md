# FACP source guidance used by LearnAble

Source: Functional Assessment Checklist for Programming (NIMH), Guidelines for Using the Checklist.

## Performance codes
- + = Yes. The child can perform the item with no help.
- C = Occasional Cueing. The child needs clues that require thinking by the child.
- VP = Verbal Prompting.
- PP = Physical Prompting.
- GP = Gestural Prompting.
- M = Modelling.
- - = No. The task has to be completely done for the child.
- NA = Not Applicable. The item is deleted from the total items to be learned when calculating percentage.
- NE = No Exposure. Lack of opportunity to learn; record it but it is not a pass.

## Quantification and progression
The checklist states that + is counted as a point. It also states that independent performance or occasional cueing (C) is considered for quantifying scores, and C can be the pass criterion for an activity while the activity is strengthened in the next level. NA is removed from the total when calculating percentage. Achievement of 80% of checklist items is the stated promotion criterion.

LearnAble therefore calculates the digital quantitative percentage as:
PASSED (+ or C) / APPLICABLE (all recorded items except NA) × 100.

Prompting codes (GP, VP, M, PP), - and NE remain observable assessment outcomes but are not passes.

## Domains
The source checklist groups functional activities under Personal, Social, Academic, Occupational and Recreational. Recreational activities are graded separately with A/B/C/D/E/NE and are not included in the quantitative promotion percentage.

## Periodicity
The source describes entry-level assessment on admission and periodic evaluation at roughly quarterly intervals, with Entry, I Term, II Term and III Term columns across a level.

## Primary-I examples
Personal activities include walking to fetch an object, stairs, opening a door, toileting, dressing, eating, drinking, washing hands/face, drying, grooming and similar daily-living activities.
Social activities include making choices, following requests, turn taking, sharing, social manners, asking for help and communication.
Academic activities include reading, writing, counting, time, money, calendar, transport and functional academic tasks.
Occupational activities include dusting, sorting vegetables, setting a table, washing plates, peeling vegetables, watering plants, sweeping and simple food preparation.
